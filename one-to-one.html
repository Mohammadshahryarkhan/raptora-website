Let's be honest — the college admission process can get really confusing.

You have your rank. Then there are cutoffs, colleges, branches, counselling rounds, fees, placements, hostels and a hundred other things to think about.

And then comes the biggest question:

“What should I actually do?”

You can search Google, watch YouTube videos and ask your friends, but sometimes you still don't get a clear answer.

That's where One-to-One Interactions at RAPTORA come in.

Have a Doubt? Just Ask.

Not every student has the same situation.

Maybe you have a good rank but aren't sure which college to choose.

Maybe you're confused between two branches.

Maybe you don't understand how counselling works.

Or maybe you've simply reached the point where you're thinking:

“I have no idea what I'm supposed to do next.”

That's completely okay.

You can talk to someone, explain your situation and get guidance based on your actual needs.

You can discuss things like:

  • Which college should I consider?
  • Which branch would be better for me?
  • What colleges can I get with my rank?
  • How does counselling work?
  • How should I arrange my choices?
  • What should I do after seat allotment?
  • Which college has better placements?
  • Should I choose college A or college B?
  • What should I keep in mind before taking admission?

Sometimes, having someone patiently explain things makes everything feel much simpler.

Start With a Simple Google Form

Want to talk to a mentor?

You can start by filling out a RAPTORA Google Form.

You'll be able to share some basic information about yourself and what you're looking for help with.

For example:

  • Your name
  • Entrance exam
  • Rank or score
  • Course you're interested in
  • Colleges you're considering
  • Your main doubts
  • Preferred time for a session

This helps us understand what you actually need before the conversation begins.

No complicated process.

Just tell us what's confusing you.

Talk Face-to-Face Through Google Meet

Sometimes texting isn't enough.

You might have five questions, then think of three more while you're talking.

That's why one-to-one sessions can happen through Google Meet.

You can join from your phone or laptop and have an actual conversation with a mentor.

You can show them your options, explain what you're confused about and ask questions as they come to you.

You don't have to prepare some perfect speech.

Just come with your doubts.

We'll take it from there.

Not Just One Question — Complete Guidance

One-to-one guidance isn't only about answering:

“Which college should I choose?”

There's a lot more to the admission journey.

We can help you understand the process step by step.

Your Rank

Got your result but don't know what your rank actually means for your college options? Let's talk about it.

Choosing a College

Have a list of colleges but can't decide which ones should be your priority? We can help you compare your options and understand what matters.

Choosing a Branch

CSE? AIML? ECE? Mechanical? Something completely different?

Choosing a branch can be a huge decision, and it's okay to have questions before committing to one.

Counselling

Counselling can feel like a completely different language when you're seeing it for the first time.

Rounds, choice filling, allotment, upgrades, documents...

We'll help you understand what each step means.

Choice Filling

This is one of those parts where students often think:

“What if I arrange my choices wrong?”

You can discuss your preferences and understand how to approach your choice list more carefully.

After Seat Allotment

Got a seat?

Great — but what now?

You may still have questions about reporting, documents, fees, upgrades or what happens next.

That's where guidance can help again.

Maybe you're sitting there thinking:

“I have this one question, but surely someone else has already asked it.”

Probably.

That's why RAPTORA can also have live Q&A sessions where students can ask questions and hear answers in real time.

You can ask about:

  • College admissions

No Question Is Too Small

Sometimes students don't ask questions because they think they'll sound silly.

Please don't worry about that.

If you're wondering:

“What happens after this?”

Ask.

If you're thinking:

“I don't understand how this works.”

Ask.

If you're stuck between two choices:

Talk about it.

You're going through this process for the first time. You're not supposed to know everything already.
